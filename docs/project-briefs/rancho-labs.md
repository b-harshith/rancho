# RanchoLabs Market Intelligence Project

## Overview

RanchoLabs wanted to understand where expansion would make the most sense, especially from a school-market and family-demand perspective. The hard part was that the answer could not come from one dataset. School density, student enrollment, premium school concentration, residential depth, office presence, hospitals, and locality signals all had to be compared together.

So the project became a multi-city market intelligence portal. The data was structured across five major layers: schools, residential real estate, offices, hospitals, and localities. These were mapped geographically and compared at city, zone, micro-market, and H3 cell level.

The portal helped answer questions like: which city should Rancho explore next, which zones have stronger demand signals, and which micro-markets should be validated physically before any business decision is made.

## Approach

The project was designed as a decision-support workflow, not a final forecasting engine. I tried to keep the research honest by separating direct evidence from derived or modeled evidence.

The major inputs were:

- School records and school-market demand indicators
- Residential real estate and project/unit depth
- Office-space and employment anchor context
- Hospital and locality context
- Geospatial zones, micro-markets, and H3 map cells

## My role

My contribution was on the market research and analytical structuring side: organizing the data layers, helping convert raw signals into comparable views, and presenting the findings through a live portal.

I used AI tools and an AI-assisted IDE throughout the process to speed up exploration, understand unfamiliar code/data paths, structure the workflow, and iterate on the final output. The main value of the project for me was learning how to move from a broad business question to a practical research system that could support a real decision conversation.

## What I focused on

- Turning an ambiguous expansion question into a structured market-research framework
- Comparing multiple data layers without treating every signal as equally reliable
- Making the output useful for shortlisting, discussion, and on-ground validation
- Using AI-assisted workflows to iterate faster while still checking the business logic

## What I learned

This project taught me how important framing is in market research. A large dataset by itself does not create a good recommendation. The harder part is deciding which signals matter, where the data is strong, where it is only directional, and how to present the result without overstating certainty.
