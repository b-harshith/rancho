Geospatial Methodology

Welcome to the interactive market research explainer for Bengaluru. This tool visualizes the exact, step-by-step mathematical and spatial pipeline used to evaluate locations, estimate target addressable markets (TAM), and determine optimal center launch points.

Step 2 of 7

Double-Quartile Filtering

To isolate target customers, raw datasets are filtered using a Double-Quartile Process.

First, raw listings are split into pricing quartiles (Q1–Q4). Only listings in the 4th quartile (Q4 - top 25% premium bracket) are retained. A secondary Q1-Q4 distribution is run exclusively on this premium subset to classify them into luxury tiers.

Watch the map. Non-premium points fade into the background, leaving only the premium subsets color-coded by their luxury level.

Toggling these categories displays their spatial clusters. Ultra Luxury housing (5Cr+ per unit) is highly concentrated in central and eastern belts.

Step 3 of 7

Q4 School Market Evidence

We clean and deduplicate the school universe, then recompute Q4 as the top 25% of school entities by maximum annual fee.

The evidence remains attached to each school entity:

  • Grades 2–9 enrollment: Shown as UDISE-backed or estimated.
  • Physical campus: Used for mapping without assigning students to residential hexes.
Q4 = top floor(N / 4) cleaned entities ranked by fee_max

The map shows Q4 school entities and their enrollment evidence. Location decisions use separate live catchment and residential ledgers.

Step 4 of 7

Income Mapping & TAM Derivation

To estimate the target addressable market (TAM), we calculate the estimated number of affluent families per society based on its size (unit count) and luxury category.

Our core target is families with **household income of ₹50L+**. To estimate this, we apply weighting factors:

TAM = Ultra + Super + Luxury + Premium + (0.4 * Aspirational)

We include 100% of units from Premium and above, plus a conservative 40% of units from the Aspirational Premium band (₹25L–₹50L) to account for households crossing the ₹40L+ threshold.

On the map, society points are now scaled in size proportional to their derived family TAM count. Larger circles represent massive luxury societies (e.g. 2,000+ units).

Step 5 of 7

H3 Hexagon Aggregation

Plotting raw points is not enough for spatial statistics. We overlay Uber's **H3 spatial index** at **Resolution 7** (cells of ~5.16 sq km area).

Watch the animation: points stream their TAM values into the nearest hexagon centroid. The cells then light up as a heatmap representing the aggregated total family TAM.

Step 6 of 7

Zone Aggregation & Priorities

Next, we roll up individual H3 cells into the **9 major macro zones** of Bangalore to formulate high-level business decisions.

The final output recommends "Launch Now" (green pulsing zones), "Shortlist" (blue zones), or "Avoid/Deprioritize" (grey zones).

The South-East and East zones carry a dominant 46.0% combined share of Bangalore's total target family TAM, making them the primary launch markets.

Step 7 of 7

Catchments & Route Finding

When selecting specific commercial sites, we calculate **travel catchments** and **route-based accessibility scores**.

Rather than drawing simple circles, we trace route paths along the OpenStreetMap road network. We compute travel distances and travel times using a local OpenSource Routing Machine (OSRM) graph.

Watch the map: we have placed a center in **Chikkakanneli (South-East)**. We draw a 7km drive catchment circle and trace the OSRM route paths (magenta neon lines) to the nearest luxury societies and premium schools.
